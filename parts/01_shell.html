How this works

You don't just watch it. You run the farm while it happens.

Save the coop
Two hens. Three waves. Larger starting flocks attract larger raids; the setup forecast shows each wave. Enemy numbers are set at the start, so reinforcements do not increase them. Stop every scheduled predator in the final wave to win. A breach is a rescue opportunity: the run ends only when both hens are lost. Your surviving defenders and damaged fences carry into the next wave.
Regroup
Between waves, choose one reward: repair the run, recruit six geese, or add supplies to the war chest. You can change your selection, including while paused. Your latest choice takes effect when the countdown ends. If you leave it unselected, you receive supplies. The wave banner previews the next approach.
Deploy
Choose a reinforcement, then tap the field to set its arrival point. Help takes a moment to arrive, so plan ahead. You can also select a named farm edge using the arrival controls.
Daily raid
Today's raid gives everyone the same starting challenge. After your run, Challenge a friend copies a fresh attempt at that raid; Copy link shares your recorded battle.
Other modes
Coop defense is a single custom raid. Open battle is the original army-versus-army simulator. Both remain available in the matchup settings.
4  Repair
Repair half of the weakest fence section, even after a breach. It recharges in 20 seconds. The status above the field shows the weakest fence and how many hens remain.
5  Rally
For 8 seconds, defenders within 24 metres of the coop regroup at the weakest fence or pursue intruders inside. They still fight enemies in reach. Recharges in 22 seconds. Each hen’s health bar shows how much time you have to rescue her.
Replay
After the battle, choose a timestamp under Moments to replay to watch from three seconds before that event.
War chest
Points build during combat. Spend them on the animals along the bottom to call help mid-battle. Geese shove enemies, dogs intercept quickly, and a bull breaks through a crowd. Saving up changes what you can call next.
1  Horn
Everything on your side swings 35% faster and hits 15% harder for six seconds. Tempo. Use it when the lines are already locked and you want to win the exchange.
2  Feed
Drops a pile mid-field. Birds break off and converge on it, and your whole side stops routing for seven seconds. It's a rally and a regroup at once — the answer to a collapse.
3  Light
Predators do 45% less damage and swing 35% slower for five seconds — but only in the dark. In daylight it manages about a third of that, because that is what switching on a floodlight at noon is worth. The night coop is where it earns its cooldown.
Call it
Pick a side on the matchup card. It locks when the countdown hits zero and the verdict tells you whether you were right. Most people are confident and wrong.
Camera
Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom. Auto Cam hands it back to the director, which follows the front of the flock and then the clash.
Copy link
Every fight has a seed. The link on the verdict card replays that exact battle for anyone who opens it — same spawns, same result. That's how you make someone argue with you about it.
R  B  H
Reel crops to 9:16 for video. Blood is off by default and toggles on. H hides the interface for a clean recording. Space starts or pauses a fight. Tactical gives you a steady overhead view. Choose a playback speed next to Pause.

Judge Dean LLC presents

Small flock.
Big trouble.

A family of raccoons raided the coop in the middle of the night.

Eenie and Moe didn't make it.

So this is a simulator built to answer the question that comes after it: how many chickens would it have taken?

Two hens. Three waves. One very angry farm.

Eenie and Moe

We kept eight hens in the yard behind the house. My son named two of them Eenie and Moe — he was three when they arrived, and those were the two he could tell apart.

They were his. He'd carry them around the yard with their legs dangling, narrating their opinions in a voice he invented for them. Eenie was the bold one. Moe followed her everywhere and complained about it the whole way.

A family of raccoons got into the coop in the middle of the night. By the time I got out there with a flashlight it was already over.

Eenie and Moe didn't make it.

He took it harder than I was ready for. It was his first real loss, and there was no version of that morning I could fix for him — no explanation that made it land any softer. He just kept asking me the same thing, over and over, the way kids do when they're trying to find a door out of it:

“How many chickens would it have taken?”

I didn't know. Nobody knows. It isn't the kind of question that has an answer sitting around waiting for you.

But I'm a nerdy dad who writes code at the kitchen table, and it turns out that when your kid asks you an impossible question, you can either say “I don't know” or you can spend a few weeks building the machine that finds out. So that's what this is.

Every bird in here fights like a real bird. The hens are brave and outmatched. The roosters have spurs and know how to use them. The raccoons are quick and clever and they do not lose to small numbers. Nothing is rigged in the flock's favour, because a rigged answer wouldn't have meant anything to him.

Somewhere in those numbers is the flock that would have been enough. He found it on a Sunday afternoon, sitting on my lap, and he laughed for the first time in about a week.

The rest of the hens are fine, by the way. The coop has a proper latch now, and a light on a sensor. We're getting two more in the spring. He's already picked the names.

— for Eenie and Moe
